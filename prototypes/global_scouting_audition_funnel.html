03 — Cross-label visibility gap
Today — four separate funnels
  • ✕ JYP and HYBE both scouting the same Southeast Asia applicant pool independently
  • ✕ A top applicant who declines HYBE's offer is not flagged to JYP automatically
  • ✕ No shared view of which regions are producing the highest quality applicants vs. which have high volume but low pass rates
  • ✕ Stage 3 drop-off reasons unshared — all four labels re-learn the same regional market characteristics independently
With this layer
  • ✓ One funnel view across all participating labels — shared at the aggregate stage level, not individual applicant data
  • ✓ Regional pass-rate benchmarks visible across labels — so each A&R team calibrates against the same market reality
  • ✓ Applicant privacy maintained — no individual applicant data crosses label boundaries; only aggregate stage counts
  • ✓ Shared regional intelligence: SE Asia producing highest quality applicants at Stage 3 — all four labels can weight their scouting budgets accordingly
⚠️ The pipeline is shrinking — the window to move is narrowing Industry reporting indicates that top-tier applicants — those who pass Stage 3 consistently — are receiving offers from multiple labels simultaneously and from independent agencies. The 70% acceptance rate shown above is illustrative; real acceptance rates have reportedly declined as alternatives to the Big 4 trainee model grow. Earlier identification and faster offer processes are the only structural response.
✅ What this layer does NOT do This layer tracks aggregate funnel metrics only — stage counts, regional pass rates, offer-to-sign ratios. It does not track, store, or share individual applicant identities, evaluations, or scores across labels. Each label's A&R assessments remain private. The shared view is: how many, from where, at what stage — not who or what was said about them.
